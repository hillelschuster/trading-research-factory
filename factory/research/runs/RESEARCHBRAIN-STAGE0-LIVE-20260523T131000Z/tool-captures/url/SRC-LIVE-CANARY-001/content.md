<h2>Trading breakouts and fakeouts</h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Breakout trading is a popular trading strategy followed by many traders and trading <strong>algorithms. </strong></span><span style="font-weight: 400;">There are two ways you can spot a breakout, the first one is by using price action, and the second is by using technical indicators that measure volatility.</span></p>
<p><strong>What is volatility? </strong></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Volatility measures price fluctuations over certain time periods. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">When there is high volatility, markets go back and forth very quickly; when there is low volatility, markets are trading in tight ranges. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">During times of low volatility, we can expect breakouts to occur. Bollinger Bands, Keltner or Donchian Channels are the most popular indicators to measure volatility</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">They measure volatility based on different indicators such as moving averages or Average true range. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Another way of looking at volatility is by using price action. You can u</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">se horizontal support and resistance or different chart patterns to spot a possible breakout. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Breakout trading can be considered an impatient approach as breakout traders often use stop or market orders to chase rising volatility.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This can work, but very often, we can see false breaks above and below existing ranges. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">These false breaks happen for one simple reason, above and below every easily recognizable price range, you can find two types of orders. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">If we take a look at an example of horizontal resistance, which was already tested in the past, above it, we can find stop-loss orders from traders who are already </span>short and also buy stop orders from traders that are anticipating a breakout.</p>
<p><img fetchpriority="high" decoding="async" class="alignnone wp-image-3157" src="https://academy.ftmo.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/example2.png" alt="" width="1121" height="587" /></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Because both of these essentially buy orders, they bring a large amount of liquidity, in other words resting orders, to the market. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Large market participants often use this liquidity and absorb all the buy orders with their large sells. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">This will result in a false breakout above the resistance and continuation down. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Not becoming a victim of these false breakouts is not that hard; all you need to do is not put your stop-loss to obvious places where there is a high likelihood </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">of other traders having their stop-loss. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Another thing is trying to have a little more patient approach rather than having a “fear of missing out”, and after you see a breakout outside of any tight range, </span>wait to see if new prices will be accepted or not.</p>
<p><img decoding="async" class="alignnone wp-image-3158" src="https://academy.ftmo.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Example3a.png" alt="" width="1115" height="584" /></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Of course, there will be situations where you will miss out on the breakout, but more often than not, waiting for the breakout and placing a limit order to an area where the breakout occurred can be a smarter play.</span></p>
