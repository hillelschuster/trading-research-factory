            <h1 class="lesson__title">
                Forex Trading Hours and Sessions            </h1>
            <hr />
            <div class="lesson__content bot-150">
                <p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Forex is a unique market because of its high liquidity, and size and because it is traded <strong>24 hours a day 5 days a week</strong>. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Because of that, traders can trade at any time of the day that suits them and their schedule</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">It doesn&#8217;t matter if you are an early bird or you like to stay long awake during the night. One thing is sure; the forex market will always be open. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Just not at weekends.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">But there is a little catch.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Although we can trade at any time we want, there are certain nuances and characteristics for different times of day. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">For example as we travel from Asia to Europe, we need to know what time is there and how many hours we have to add or subtract on our watches. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">To not cause any confusion, a</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">ll the times that we will mention in this video, we are going to base on London time with GMT+0. </span></p>

<h2><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Trading_hours"></span><b>Trading hours</b><span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Every week on Sunday, the Forex market opens at <strong>9 PM</strong> GMT, which makes the start of the Sydney session.  </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Two hours later, the Tokyo session starts at <strong>11 PM</strong>. To ease things up, we can merge these together and call them an  </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Asian session (sometimes called Tokyo session)</strong>. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Asian session ends at <strong>8 AM. </strong></span><span style="font-weight: 400;">In Europe, we have Frankfurt and London sessions with <strong>Frankfurt</strong> starting at <strong>7 AM</strong> and <strong>London</strong> starting at <strong>8 AM</strong>.  </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">But once again to make things easier, we will call it a <strong>European session (sometimes called the London session)</strong>. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">At <strong>12 PM</strong> <strong>North American session</strong> <strong>(sometimes called the </strong><strong>New York session) </strong>starts and it goes alongside the </span><strong>European</strong> session <strong>until 4 PM. </strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is called the <strong>European/North American </strong><strong>overlap</strong>, and it usually brings a lot of trading opportunities as there </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">is the highest volume traded during those hours. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">After London closes at 4 PM, the New York runs till 9 PM, and once it is completed, the Asian session starts again </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">and the whole cycle repeats. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">As time changes during the year, there are also slight changes to trading hours, so we should always keep up with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">what&#8217;s happening.</span></p>
<p><img fetchpriority="high" decoding="async" class="alignnone wp-image-2948 " src="https://academy.ftmo.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Group-2340.png" alt="" width="887" height="282" /></p>
<h2><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Asian_session"></span><b>Asian session</b><span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Asian session has <strong>the lowest volume</strong> of the three trading sessions. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">But this doesn&#8217;t mean that we cannot find any opportunities there. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Besides traders that live in Asia, this trading session can also attract European traders who like to trade during </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">the night or Americans that just got home from work and want to spend late afternoon trading. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Asian session is mostly controlled by the Japanese economy, the third biggest globally</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">The <strong>Japanese yen is the third most traded currency,</strong> with around 20% of all transactions. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">During the Asian session, around 20% of the daily volume is traded. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Besides Japan, we have countries such as Singapore, New Zealand and Australia which participate in the market. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Because of that, currency pairs such as <strong>AUDJPY</strong>, <strong>AUDNZD</strong> or <strong>NZDJPY</strong> are the best ones to trade. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Macroeconomic releases from New Zealand, Australia and Japan also cause high volatility, which every trader should pay attention to.</span></p>
<h2><span class="ez-toc-section" id="European_session_London"></span><b>European session </b><b>(London)</b><span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">London was always one of the most important business centres globally due to its strategic place between Europe and America. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nowadays, London is one of the leading financial hubs in the world. That&#8217;s why the European session is called the </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">London session because the city accounts for the majority of total business volume. The European session makes up over 30% of traded volume alone at the Forex market</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">One of the key reasons to trade during the European session is that we can trade both European and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">American macroeconomics releases, which bring significant volatility to the markets. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Thanks to high liquidity, traders can benefit from very low spreads optimal for scalpers and day traders. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Since the European session is the first high liquid session of the day, <strong>many new trends are forming during the London open</strong>. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Markets usually move right after the open and slow down during the London lunch hours as large participants </span>take small breaks before New York kicks in. <span style="font-weight: 400;">During the European session, not only are <strong>major currencies moving</strong>, but we often see high volatility </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">in minors as well. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">The currency pair <strong>EURGBP</strong>, and <strong>GBPJPY</strong> are extremely popular crosses that offer high volatility and </span>plenty of trading opportunities.</p>
<h2><span class="ez-toc-section" id="North_American_session_New_York"></span><b>North American session</b><b> (New York) </b><span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The North American session is the last trading session for the given day. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">North American session is sometimes called New York because of the city&#8217;s importance as the world&#8217;s financial hub.</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Because <strong>over 80% of the traded volume involves the US dollar</strong>, there is no wonder that the </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">North American session brings a significant amount of volatility every day. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Also, most of the key fundamental events happen during the North American session. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Namely, these can be the <strong>non-farm payrolls &#8211; NFP, Federal Open Market Committee &#8211; FOMC,  </strong></span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>unemployment rates or </strong></span><strong>consumer price index &#8211; CPI. </strong><span style="font-weight: 400;">North American session slows down around <strong>London close at 4 PM,</strong> but we can find opportunities </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">in the market even after that. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Although money usually flows to the US index market once the European session is finished, such as S&amp;P500, Dow Jones or Nasdaq. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Same as the European session, the North American session offers tight spreads and the right conditions for intraday trading.</span></p>
<h2><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Days_of_the_week"></span><b>Days of the week</b><span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Now that we covered separate sessions let&#8217;s look at different days of the week. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Markets open on Sunday evening London time, but there is usually a minimal volume during that time. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Monday morning also tends to be slow until the North American session starts and markets really start to pick up. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Tuesday</strong> usually offers double the volatility of Monday, and it makes it one of the <strong>best days to trade </strong></span>during the week, with <strong>Wednesday and Thursday</strong>. <span style="font-weight: 400;">Fridays are interesting days as they have lower volumes, but there are many macroeconomic events during Friday. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">For example, NFP, which is every first Friday of the month, can offer many opportunities, but some </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">traders prefer to skip those entirely, as markets tend to be less predictable during these events. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">After London closes on Friday, volumes usually dry up.</span></p>
<h2><span class="ez-toc-section" id="Trading_hours_of_CFDs"></span><b>Trading hours of CFDs</b><span class="ez-toc-section-end"></span></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Besides currency pairs, many brokers offer CFDs for indices and commodities to trade as well. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">For equity indices, they differ based on the country they are from. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>European indices</strong> such as DAX or Stoxx <strong>open at 1 AM and trade until 10 PM London time</strong>. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>US indices</strong> such as SP500, Nasdaq or Dow Jones <strong>trade 23 hours a day</strong> with a break </span>between 10 PM and 11 PM. <span style="font-weight: 400;">But for US indices, we have something that is called a <strong>pit session</strong>. The name</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> of pit sessions comes from trading pits, representing the time period from <strong>2:30 PM till market close. </strong></span><span style="font-weight: 400;">These are hours when US traders are awake, bringing large amounts of volumes to the market. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Commodities such as <strong>crude oil and gold</strong> trade <strong>23 hours a day</strong> with the highest volume during </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">the North American session due to trading in the futures market.</span></p>

    const footerNewsletterSend = () => {
        if (emailInput.value !== "") {
            const xhr = new XMLHttpRequest()
            const baseUrl = "https://api.elasticemail.com/v2/contact/add"
            const publicAccountID = "c555beab-7a7a-4882-a50d-eaef7c4a7f66"
